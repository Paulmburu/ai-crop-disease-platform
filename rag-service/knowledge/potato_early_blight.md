# Potato early blight

## Overview
Early blight is a common fungal leaf disease of potato. It mostly attacks older leaves and can also cause a dry rot of tubers.

## Cause
Potato early blight is caused by the fungus Alternaria solani.

## Symptoms
- Leaves: circular to angular dark brown spots about 3-4 mm across with concentric rings that look like a target or bull's-eye, usually starting on older, lower leaves.
- Badly affected leaves turn yellow and drop.
- Tubers: dark, slightly sunken lesions with a brown, corky dry rot underneath.

## Conditions that favour it
Warm weather with moisture from dew, rain or irrigation. Alternating wet and dry periods, with dry windy spells, help spread the spores. Stressed plants (poor nutrition, other pests) are more susceptible.

## How it spreads
The fungus survives between seasons on potato debris, in soil, on tubers and on related plants such as other solanaceous crops and weeds. Spores spread by wind and water splash; tubers can be infected at harvest as they pass through contaminated soil.

## Management
- Keep plants vigorous with balanced fertilisation, good irrigation and pest control.
- Rotate away from potatoes and tomatoes and remove infected debris.
- Later-maturing varieties tend to be less affected.
- Scout regularly; fungicides (for example chlorothalonil, azoxystrobin or boscalid) are applied when symptoms first appear and repeated every 7-10 days if justified. Follow the product label and local regulations.

## Look-alikes
Late blight lesions are larger, water-soaked and greasy-looking, and show white growth on the leaf underside in humid weather; early blight lesions are dry with concentric rings.

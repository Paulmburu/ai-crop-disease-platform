# Tomato late blight

## Overview
Late blight can kill tomato foliage and rot fruit within days in cool, humid weather. The same pathogen attacks potatoes.

## Cause
Phytophthora infestans, a water mould (oomycete).

## Symptoms
- Leaves: small water-soaked areas that rapidly enlarge into purple-brown, oily-looking blotches.
- Greyish-white fuzzy growth on the leaf underside around lesions.
- Whole leaves die; infection moves into petioles and stems.
- Fruit: firm, brown, greasy-looking areas, often starting on the shoulders of the fruit.

## Conditions that favour it
Humidity above about 90% with average temperatures around 16-26 °C (60-78 °F); under these conditions infection can occur in about 10 hours.

## How it spreads
Survives on infected potatoes (including volunteers and cull piles), tomatoes and hairy nightshade. Spores spread readily by wind, so outbreaks can move between farms.

## Management
- Plant resistant tomato varieties where available.
- Remove volunteer tomatoes, potatoes and nightshade; inspect transplants.
- Avoid sprinkler irrigation.
- Apply protectant fungicides before disease appears in high-risk weather, at 5-7 day intervals; registered options include chlorothalonil, mancozeb, azoxystrobin, dimethomorph and famoxadone + cymoxanil. Rotate modes of action and follow the label and local regulations.
- Remove and destroy infected plants promptly.

## Look-alikes
Leaf mold also shows growth on the leaf underside, but it is olive-green and the spots on top are yellow, not dark and greasy.

# Tomato Septoria leaf spot

## Overview
Septoria leaf spot is a very common fungal disease that defoliates tomato plants from the bottom up, especially in wet seasons.

## Cause
The fungus Septoria lycopersici.

## Symptoms
- Many small, circular spots (less than about 3 mm / 1/8 inch) on the lower leaves first, with dark brown edges and lighter tan to grey centres.
- Tiny black dots (pycnidia, which hold spores) in the centre of spots.
- Spots merge, leaves yellow, wither and drop, moving up the plant.
- Fruit is rarely affected.

## Conditions that favour it
Moderate to warm temperatures with high humidity and plenty of rain; moisture is essential for spore release.

## How it spreads
Survives on infected tomato debris and solanaceous weeds such as horsenettle. Spores spread mainly by rain splash, and also by workers, tools and insects moving through wet foliage.

## Management
- Rotate away from tomatoes for at least 2 years.
- Remove crop debris and control solanaceous weeds.
- Stake and prune for airflow; mulch to reduce soil splash.
- Avoid working in plants when they are wet; remove infected lower leaves early.
- Preventive fungicides such as chlorothalonil, copper or mancozeb. Follow the product label and local regulations.

## Look-alikes
Early blight spots are larger with concentric rings and no tiny black dots; bacterial spot lesions are water-soaked and lack pycnidia.

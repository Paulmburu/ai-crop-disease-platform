# Tomato target spot

## Overview
Target spot is a fungal disease of tomato in warm, wet climates that causes leaf spots, defoliation and fruit lesions.

## Cause
The fungus Corynespora cassiicola, which also attacks papaya, cucumber, soybean and many other plants.

## Symptoms
- Older leaves first: irregular brown spots with yellow margins that enlarge up to about 10 mm and show concentric rings (a target pattern).
- Leaves yellow, collapse and fall; spread can be rapid in wet, windy weather.
- Lesions on stems and fruit; fruit spots can be sunken.

## Conditions that favour it
Warm, wet weather with frequent rain and high humidity.

## How it spreads
Spores are spread by wind-blown rain; if windy, wet weather continues for a few days, spread is fast. The fungus survives on crop residue and alternative hosts.

## Management
- Inspect seedlings; do not plant new crops next to old infected ones, or near papaya.
- Prune lower leaves for airflow and remove leaves with symptoms.
- Control weeds, avoid overhead irrigation, destroy crop residue after harvest.
- Rotate crops for about 3 years.
- Fungicides such as chlorothalonil, copper or mancozeb, started when spots first appear and repeated every 10-14 days, covering both leaf surfaces. Follow the product label and local regulations.

## Look-alikes
Early blight also shows ringed lesions; early target spot lesions can resemble bacterial spot.

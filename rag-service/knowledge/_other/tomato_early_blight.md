# Tomato early blight

## Overview
Early blight is a fungal disease of tomato leaves, stems and fruit, usually starting on the oldest leaves.

## Cause
The fungus Alternaria solani (closely related Alternaria species are also reported).

## Symptoms
- Leaves: small dark brown lesions, about 6-12 mm, with concentric rings (a target pattern), often surrounded by yellow tissue; lower leaves first.
- Stems: dark, leathery lesions; on seedlings the stem base can rot (collar rot).
- Fruit: dark, sunken, leathery spots with rings, usually near the stem (calyx) end.

## Conditions that favour it
Several days of humid weather after rain, warm temperatures and free moisture on leaves. Disease slows in hot, dry weather.

## How it spreads
Survives in soil on residue of infected tomatoes, potatoes and nightshade weeds. Spores spread by wind and splashing water and need free moisture to infect.

## Management
- Remove volunteer tomatoes, potatoes and nightshade weeds.
- Rotate crops so infected residue can decompose; remove or bury debris.
- Mulch and stake plants so leaves stay off the soil and dry faster.
- Fungicides such as chlorothalonil, mancozeb, fixed copper or Bacillus subtilis products are applied when symptoms first appear in favourable weather. Follow the product label and local regulations.

## Look-alikes
Target spot (Corynespora cassiicola) also forms ringed lesions; Septoria leaf spot lesions are much smaller with tiny black dots in the centre.

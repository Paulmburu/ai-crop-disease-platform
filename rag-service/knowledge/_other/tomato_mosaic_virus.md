# Tomato mosaic virus (ToMV)

## Overview
Tomato mosaic is caused by very stable viruses that spread on hands and tools and can persist in plant debris for years.

## Cause
Tomato mosaic virus (ToMV) and the closely related tobacco mosaic virus (TMV), both tobamoviruses.

## Symptoms
- Light and dark green mottling (mosaic) on leaves.
- Leaf distortion, including narrow, fern-like leaves; in cool weather leaves can become very narrow (shoestring-like).
- Possible stunting, uneven ripening and internal or surface browning of fruit.

## Conditions that favour it
Frequent handling of plants (transplanting, pruning, tying), contaminated tools and infected seed or debris.

## How it spreads
Mechanically: on hands, clothing, tools and equipment, and from infected seed, plant debris and tobacco products. It is not spread by insects.

## Management
- Use tested or treated seed and healthy transplants.
- Strict sanitation: wash hands with soap before handling plants, disinfect tools, do not smoke or handle tobacco near plants.
- Minimise handling; handle healthy plants before suspect ones.
- Remove infected plants and debris.
- Grow varieties with genetic resistance to tobamoviruses.

## Look-alikes
Other viruses (cucumber mosaic, potyviruses) and some nutrient deficiencies cause mottling; lab tests distinguish them.

# Bacterial spot of bell pepper

## Overview
Bacterial spot is one of the most common leaf and fruit diseases of bell pepper. It defoliates plants, which exposes fruit to sunscald, and the fruit spots make peppers unmarketable.

## Cause
Bacteria in the genus Xanthomonas. Four species cause bacterial spot of pepper and tomato: Xanthomonas euvesicatoria, X. vesicatoria, X. perforans and X. gardneri (older references call it Xanthomonas campestris pv. vesicatoria). On pepper, X. euvesicatoria is the most common.

## Symptoms
- Leaves: small, water-soaked spots, often angular and most visible on the leaf underside, that turn brown and scab-like (about 6 mm / 0.25 inch).
- Infected leaves yellow and drop early.
- Fruit: small raised blister-like spots that become brown, cracked, rough and wart-like.
- Stems: elongated raised cankers.

## Conditions that favour it
Warm, wet weather: high humidity and long periods of leaf wetness. Development is favoured by temperatures of about 24-30 °C (75-86 °F) and frequent rain. Symptoms appear 5-15 days after infection.

## How it spreads
The bacteria survive on and in seed, in infected transplants, volunteer plants and crop debris. They spread by wind-driven rain, overhead irrigation splash, and by handling or working among wet plants.

## Management
- Start clean: certified pathogen-free seed and disease-free transplants.
- Rotate away from peppers and tomatoes (other solanaceous crops) for at least one year, longer where possible.
- Use drip or furrow irrigation instead of overhead sprinklers; avoid working in wet fields.
- Remove volunteers and crop debris.
- Copper sprays, alone or with mancozeb, can slow spread when applied before or early in an outbreak, but copper-resistant strains are common, so copper often gives only partial control. Always follow the product label and local regulations.

## Look-alikes
Early fungal leaf spots can look similar. Bacterial spot lesions are small, water-soaked at first and do not show concentric rings; a lab can confirm bacteria streaming from cut tissue.

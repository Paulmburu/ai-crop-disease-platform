# Tomato leaf mold

## Overview
Leaf mold is mainly a disease of tomatoes grown in greenhouses and high tunnels, where humidity stays high.

## Cause
The fungus Passalora fulva (older names: Fulvia fulva, Cladosporium fulvum).

## Symptoms
- Upper leaf surface: pale green to yellow spots.
- Lower leaf surface: olive-green to greyish, velvety mould under the spots.
- Spots can merge and kill leaves when infection is severe; older leaves are affected first.
- Occasionally a black, leathery rot at the stem end of fruit.

## Conditions that favour it
Relative humidity above about 85%, with optimal development around 22-24 °C (72-75 °F).

## How it spreads
Spores spread by air movement, water splash, tools and workers. The fungus survives on crop residue.

## Management
- Lower humidity: ventilate, heat at night in greenhouses if needed, and space and prune plants for airflow.
- Avoid overhead irrigation and wet leaves.
- Remove lower and infected leaves promptly; clear residue after the crop.
- Grow resistant varieties. Several races of the fungus exist, and each resistant variety protects against only some of them.
- Preventive fungicides are used where the disease is common; follow the product label and local regulations.

## Look-alikes
Late blight (dark greasy lesions, white growth) and grey mould (Botrytis); leaf mold has yellow spots on top with olive-green mould below.

# Tomato bacterial spot

## Overview
Bacterial spot causes leaf spots, defoliation and scabby fruit on tomato. It is difficult to control once established in warm, wet weather.

## Cause
Xanthomonas bacteria: X. perforans, X. euvesicatoria, X. vesicatoria and X. gardneri. X. perforans is the most common on tomato in many regions.

## Symptoms
- Leaves: small circular, water-soaked spots, sometimes with a yellow halo, usually less than 3 mm across; spots can merge into larger blighted areas.
- Fruit: small raised spots that become scabby.
- Defoliation exposes fruit to sunscald.

## Conditions that favour it
Temperatures around 24-30 °C (75-86 °F), frequent rain and long leaf-wetness periods.

## How it spreads
Survives in seed, transplants, volunteer plants and crop debris. Spread by wind-driven rain, irrigation splash and handling wet plants; in greenhouses with overhead watering it can move about 2 metres in three weeks.

## Management
- Use certified pathogen-free seed and healthy transplants; seed can be treated with hot water or dilute bleach.
- Avoid overhead irrigation and working among wet plants.
- Rotate with non-solanaceous crops and remove debris and volunteers.
- Preventive copper-based sprays, often combined with mancozeb, help limit spread; copper resistance is common. Follow the product label and local regulations.

## Look-alikes
Early target spot (Corynespora) can start as similar dark pinpoint lesions; bacterial spot lacks concentric rings.

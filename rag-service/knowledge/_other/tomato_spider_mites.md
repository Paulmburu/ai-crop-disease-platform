# Two-spotted spider mite on tomato

## Overview
Two-spotted spider mites are tiny pests, not a disease. They suck cell contents from leaves and can build up very quickly in hot, dry weather.

## Cause
The mite Tetranychus urticae (an arachnid, about 0.4 mm long). Adults are yellow-green to amber with two dark spots on the back; overwintering females turn red-orange.

## Symptoms
- Fine pale stippling (tiny dots) on leaves where mites have fed.
- Leaves turn yellow or bronze, become brittle and may die.
- Fine webbing on the undersides of leaves and between leaves in heavy infestations.
- Mites and their round, translucent eggs on the leaf underside (use a hand lens).

## Conditions that favour it
Hot, dry weather: in summer mites can go from egg to adult in about 5 days, so populations explode. Dusty conditions, drought-stressed plants and broad-spectrum insecticides that kill predators make outbreaks worse.

## How it spreads
Mites crawl between touching plants and are carried on wind, clothing and tools.

## Management
- Scout leaf undersides weekly in hot weather; act early.
- Keep plants well watered and reduce dust.
- Protect natural enemies such as predatory mites; avoid unnecessary broad-spectrum insecticides.
- Insecticidal soaps and horticultural oils work on contact and need repeat applications with good coverage of leaf undersides.
- If miticides are used, rotate modes of action to avoid resistance. Follow the product label and local regulations.

## Look-alikes
Stippling can be mistaken for nutrient problems or viral mottling; look for moving mites and webbing on the underside.

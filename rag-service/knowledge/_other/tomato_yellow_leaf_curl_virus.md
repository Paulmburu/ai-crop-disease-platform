# Tomato yellow leaf curl virus (TYLCV)

## Overview
Tomato yellow leaf curl is one of the most damaging tomato diseases worldwide. Plants infected young may produce almost no fruit.

## Cause
Tomato yellow leaf curl virus (TYLCV), a begomovirus (geminivirus) transmitted by whiteflies (Bemisia species).

## Symptoms
- Small leaves that curl upward and crumple.
- Yellowing between veins and along leaf margins.
- Stunted, bushy plants; flowers often drop, so fruit set falls sharply.

## Conditions that favour it
Large whitefly populations, warm weather, and nearby infected crops or weeds; tomatoes grown year-round without a break keep the virus cycling.

## How it spreads
Whiteflies carry the virus between plants over short distances. Infected transplants move it over long distances. It is not spread by touching plants.

## Management
- There is no cure for infected plants.
- Plant resistant or tolerant varieties.
- Use virus-free and whitefly-free transplants.
- Manage whiteflies; floating row covers or insect netting protect young plants.
- Remove infected plants early while infection is low, control weeds, and destroy crop residue and volunteers after harvest; a tomato-free period breaks the cycle.

## Look-alikes
Herbicide injury and nutrient deficiencies can cause curling or yellowing; whiteflies on the plant and the upward-curling, crumpled young leaves point to TYLCV.
